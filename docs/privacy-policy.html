DocDue — Privacy Policy

Last updated 30 August 2026

DocDue keeps your documents on your device and nowhere else. There is no account to create, no server to sign in to, and no copy of your data held by anyone but you.

The developer cannot read your documents. Not because of a policy promise — because they are encrypted with a key that never leaves your phone.

What DocDue stores

Everything you enter: document names, types, expiry dates, notes, and any photographs you attach.

All of it lives in an encrypted database (SQLCipher, AES‑256) inside the app's private storage. Photographs are stored inside that database rather than as ordinary image files, because a picture of a passport is the sensitive part.

The encryption key is held in the platform keystore — Android Keystore or iOS Keychain — and is marked as available on this device only. It is deliberately excluded from iCloud and Android cloud backups. One consequence is worth stating plainly: if you lose the device and have no backup file, the data cannot be recovered by anyone, including the developer. That is the intended trade.

What leaves your device

One thing, and it contains none of your documents.

When the app starts, it asks Expo's update service (u.expo.dev, operated by 650 Industries, Inc.) whether a newer version of the app's JavaScript is available. That request carries the platform, a runtime version, and an install identifier generated by Expo. It happens before the vault is opened, and it sends no document, no photograph, no date, and no passphrase.

This exists so that a security fix can reach people who would never think to check a store for an update. It is why the app holds the INTERNET permission.

There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, and no tracking of any kind. No third‑party SDK collects anything.

Backups you create

You can export your vault to a single file. That file is a separate encrypted database, locked with a passphrase you choose (PBKDF2‑HMAC‑SHA512, 256,000 iterations, AES‑256).

  • The passphrase is never stored and never transmitted. Nobody can recover it for you — if you forget it, the backup is unreadable.
  • The file is written to temporary storage and deleted as soon as you finish sharing it.
  • Once you send that file somewhere, it is subject to wherever you sent it. If you email it or put it in cloud storage, that service's privacy policy applies to the file, not this one. It remains encrypted, but its location is your choice.

Permissions, and why each exists

PermissionWhy
CameraTo photograph a document. The photo goes straight into the encrypted database.
Photos / mediaTo attach a picture you already have. Only the image you pick is read.
Biometrics / fingerprintTo unlock the app. The biometric check is performed by the operating system; the app never receives your fingerprint or face data.
NotificationsTo remind you before a document expires. Reminders are scheduled on the device.
InternetSolely the update check described above.
VibrationHaptic feedback and reminder alerts.

Two things are named here for honesty rather than necessity. The notification library the app is built on also registers Google's push‑messaging permission and a set of home‑screen badge permissions used by various Android launchers. DocDue does not operate a push server and sends no push messages — every reminder is scheduled locally on your device. Those permissions come bundled with the library and cannot be removed without losing reminders entirely.

On older versions of Android you may also see storage permissions. They are capped to Android 12 and below, where the system photo picker required them.

Children

DocDue is not directed at children under 13 and collects no information from anyone, including children.

Deleting your data

Uninstall the app. Everything goes with it — the database, the photographs, and the key in the keystore.

There is no request to submit and no account to close, because there is nothing held anywhere else to delete. Any backup files you exported and shared remain wherever you put them, and are yours to delete.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the revised version will be posted here with a new date. If a change ever means the app starts collecting something it does not collect today, that will be stated plainly rather than buried.
